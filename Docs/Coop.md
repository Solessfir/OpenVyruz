# Co-op

The port's co-op campaign, which the original does not have: up to four players through the single-player levels. Its multiplayer build ([Multiplayer.md](Multiplayer.md)) is a reference only. A level with one player plays by the original's rules, untouched; everything below applies from the second player on. The [roadmap](Roadmap.md) has what is still to come.

## In the level

Every player is a level object of the player's class with its own ship, inventory, weapons, surprises and death, all stepped by the same code as a lone player (`FLevelPlayer` in `Source/Main.cpp`). The level's bodies, actors, doors, generators, turrets and shots step once a step and meet every player (`FPlayerTargets` in `Source/Game/Engine.h`):

- **Enemies:** a seeker or tracker hunts the nearest player its sensor finds and keeps it while it can; a turret locks onto one player at a time; an actor's or a drifting body's knock hits the player nearest it. A downed player is left alone.
- **Shots and blasts:** another object's shot can strike any player, a player's shot strikes no player, and a player's blast, from a shot or from its death, hurts that player and the enemies but spares the others: there is no friendly fire. Hits, misses, kills and the fast-kill window belong to the player who fired.
- **Toughness:** the hunters' energy, shield and shield maximum are multiplied by `1 + 0.6 * (players - 1)` (`CoopHunterScale`), as the second player arrives and for every hunter born later. A saved session keeps them as a lone player meets them, so loading it toughens them afresh for the players then.

## The team

- **Money, keys and codes** are the team's, held by the first player's inventory (`FPlayerTarget::Team`, `PurseOf`): pickups, rewards, bonuses, a lift's fare and the map all draw on the one purse, and a key or code any player picks up opens its doors for everyone.
- **Packs** of energy, shield or ammo are every player's own: a pack stays in the level until each player has taken it, hidden only for those who have, so each player sees its own copy, and any money in it goes to the purse once. Money alone, keys, codes and surprises go to whoever takes them first.

## Rings

As in Sacred, every ship in a co-op level stands on a flattened ring drawn on the ground before the walls and bodies: its outer band is the shield left in red and its inner band the energy in green, as the panel's bars show them, filled clockwise from the top over a see-through dark track with a dark rim, and a downed ship's ring is all dark red. The bands are made once, three times finer than the view, and drawn with the renderer's `SoftAlpha` blend, which filters the port's own images smoothly instead of as pixel art. A lone player has none.

## Down and back

At zero energy a player is down instead of exploding: its ship coasts to a stop and pulses red, takes no command, fires nothing, collects nothing and is ignored by the enemies. A standing teammate within 100 pixels for two seconds brings it back at 30% energy. Unrevived after 30 seconds, it comes back by itself at half energy where the team last saved, or where the level started; a client's ship goes there when the host's count of its respawns changes, and the host leaves out the ships it sent before (`FCoopShip::Respawns`). With every player down at once the team is lost: each ship dies as a lone player's does and the game ends.

## Lifts

With a single player the lift leaves at once, as in the original. In co-op the lift a player enters waits until every standing player is inside it, a downed or dead one not counted, so nobody is taken along from elsewhere. Its fare and one of its uses are taken only as it leaves (`RideLift`), and any lift with a rider leaves once the whole team is in it. Every standing player keeps its energy, shield, ammo and weapons into the next level (`FCarriedPlayer::Team` on the host); a downed or dead one arrives with a fresh ship.

## Over the network

The main menu's Cooperative item hosts or joins a game. Host Game opens a lobby that lists the players as they join, its Start Game takes everyone to the first level and its Load Game on from one of the host's saved games. Join Game lists the games a broadcast finds on the LAN (UDP port 47211) and takes an address for the others; a joined player waits for the host to start, or arrives at once in a game already running. On the command line, `--host` listens on UDP port 47210 and starts at once; `--join <address>` connects to it (`Source/Net/Net.cpp` over ENet, `Source/Game/Coop.cpp` for the messages). Up to three players join, each needing the host's co-op protocol version (`CoopProtocolVersion`): a client of another version, or one more than three, is refused and told why, and a client takes none of the host's messages before the host has welcomed it with its slot. Anyone may join or leave during a level: a newcomer's ship appears beside the host's, and the hunters toughen or soften with the count.

The host's game is the game. Its level, enemies, shots, pickups, inventories, purse, downs and level changes are the truth, which it sends unreliably about twenty times a second, a snapshot with a lost fragment simply dropped (`ENET_PACKET_FLAG_UNRELIABLE_FRAGMENT`): every player, every body, the objects removed or wrecked, the packs' takers, the team's money and keys, and, reliably, every object it created, which a client creates too under its own index. Each level the host announces gets the next number (`LevelSerial`), which its snapshots and the clients' ships carry: a client takes no snapshot of another level, which could overtake the change and remove objects by index, and from the change on takes the host's objects and sends its ship only in the next level, while the host takes no ship of another level. A client hears its own pickups by the count of them the host sends. What the host would tell a client's player, its pickup texts and the down, back and lift messages, goes to that client reliably and shows there. A surprise runs on the host for every player, for what it does to the player's numbers, weapons and foes and its orbiting FX round that player's ship (`StepOrbiters`); a client learns of its own from the snapshot's count of collected surprises and runs it too, for the ship's handling, look and blinking icon. A ship is invisible on every machine as the host's copy has it (`FCoopShip::bInvisible`), and on the host any player's Time Warp slows the world. Each client moves its own ship and sends it with its fire and weapon some sixty times a second, so its own steering has no lag; the host takes that ship as it comes. The weapon in hand is the client's choice: the host's copy of its ship holds its fire with an empty weapon rather than moving on, which the client's own machine does and sends. Between snapshots a client steps its world as a lone game would, but as a puppet (`SetPuppetWorld`): it deals no damage, gives no birth, drops nothing, decides no down or death, collects nothing, saves nothing and takes no lift of its own; its shots and blasts are for show, and a body dies when the host says so. Since positions come in steps, every networked ship and body is drawn gliding from where it was drawn to the newest position over the time that position took to come, a step behind the network but without the stairs; only the drawing glides, the game keeps the positions as they come, and a jump of more than 160 pixels is drawn at once. A client follows the host to every level it starts, including a retry, and waits again when the host's game ends.

A network game never stops for one player: not for a window out of focus, which goes on silent while its ship takes no command, not for the game menu with its Options and questions or the inventory and help screens, during which every frame's network and steps still run (`AdvanceWorld`, called from `PollEvents`) and which closes by itself when the level ends under it, and not for a level's page, under which the world goes on while the reader's ship waits. The pause key holds everyone: the pause is the host's, any player's key asks for it or ends it, and the snapshots carry it. Every player may open the map, which draws on the team's purse; a client's map is not charged yet. A level the host starts from a saved game, a loaded one or a retry, comes with that session: the Level message carries its last.txt and .3do, which a client writes as its own default session in the port's data folder (`Coop\default`) and loads as the host does, as does a player joining during that level.

## Trying it

`--coopDummy` ([Tools.md](Tools.md#development-aids)) adds a second ship that follows the player, which is enough to see the rules without a second machine. Two copies of the game on one machine, one with `--host` and one with `--join 127.0.0.1`, try the network.
